Dogs

Dogs are domesticated mammals belonging to the species *Canis lupus familiaris*, and they have lived alongside humans for at least 15,000 years based on archaeological evidence. They were originally domesticated from wolves, likely through a gradual process where less aggressive individuals adapted to human environments. Today, dogs display remarkable diversity in size, behavior, and function—from small companion breeds to highly specialized working dogs such as herders, guards, and search-and-rescue animals.

One of the most significant traits of dogs is their social intelligence. Research shows that dogs can interpret human gestures, facial expressions, and even emotional tone with a high degree of accuracy compared to other animals. This ability makes them uniquely suited for roles in therapy, assistance, and companionship. For example, guide dogs assist visually impaired individuals, while therapy dogs are used in hospitals and mental health settings to reduce stress and anxiety.

In addition to their cognitive abilities, dogs require consistent care, including proper nutrition, exercise, and veterinary attention. Their lifespan varies by breed, typically ranging from 10 to 15 years. Regular physical activity is essential not only for their physical health but also for preventing behavioral issues. As a result, responsible ownership involves both meeting their basic needs and providing mental stimulation, ensuring they remain healthy and well-adjusted throughout their lives.
